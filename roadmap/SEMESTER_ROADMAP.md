# Semester Roadmap

**Programme dates:** 19 October – 14 December 2026  
**Format:** Four two-week blocks  
**Pathway:** IDEA → BUILD → PITCH → IMPACT

This is the working roadmap for the GDG on Campus TUM Hackathons & Solutions Challenge programme. It translates the programme outline into a semester sequence. Specific session dates, facilitators and venues should be confirmed by organisers in the official groups.

## Roadmap at a glance

| Block | Dates | Learning focus | Suggested workshop/activity | Milestone |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 19 Oct – 1 Nov | Hackathon foundations, problem discovery, SDGs, teams | Hackathon 101; Hack the Problem; Ideas That Matter; Build Your Dream Team | Team and problem statement |
| 2 | 2 – 15 Nov | Ideation, user journeys, MVP, technology choice | From Idea to MVP; solution sketching; project planning | MVP scope and build plan |
| 3 | 16 – 29 Nov | Build with Google, prototype, validate and iterate | Build with Google; build sessions; feedback review | Demonstrable prototype |
| 4 | 30 Nov – 14 Dec | Pitching, demos, competition readiness, sustainability | Sell Your Idea; Pitch & Demo Practice; Innovation & Entrepreneurship | Pitch, demo and next-steps plan |

## Block 1: Foundations and discovery
**19 October – 1 November**

### Goals
- Understand what hackathons are and how teams can participate.
- Identify real problems worth solving.
- Connect problem areas to relevant SDGs and intended impact.
- Find teammates and agree on working roles.

### Suggested sequence
- **Week 1 (19–25 Oct):** Programme kickoff; hackathon foundations; problem discovery; first buildathon announcements.
- **Week 2 (26 Oct–1 Nov):** Refine problem statements; SDG and beneficiary alignment; team formation and role selection.

### Milestone
Each team or individual has a concise problem statement, a target user/beneficiary, an initial impact rationale and a team/teammate plan.

## Block 2: Ideation to MVP
**2 – 15 November**

### Goals
- Explore possible solutions before choosing one.
- Describe the user journey and core user need.
- Define a realistic MVP.
- Select a suitable technical approach and plan the work.

### Suggested sequence
- **Week 3 (2–8 Nov):** Solution ideation; user journeys; sketches or wireframes; compare possible approaches.
- **Week 4 (9–15 Nov):** MVP scope; technology selection; project task board; build plan and risk review.

### Milestone
Each team has a selected concept, a clear MVP feature list, a simple solution design and a prioritised task board.

## Block 3: Build, validate and iterate
**16 – 29 November**

### Goals
- Build the core solution flow.
- Explore relevant Google developer tools where they fit the project.
- Test key assumptions and identify blockers.
- Use feedback to improve the prototype.

### Suggested sequence
- **Week 5 (16–22 Nov):** Build sprint; core functionality; short progress check-ins; unblock technical issues.
- **Week 6 (23–29 Nov):** Prototype integration; testing; user/peer feedback; prioritise improvements.

### Milestone
Each team has a demonstrable prototype or proof of concept, notes on tests and feedback, and a list of known limitations and next fixes.

## Block 4: Pitch, demo and competition readiness
**30 November – 14 December**

### Goals
- Communicate the problem and solution clearly.
- Show the prototype reliably.
- Prepare presentation materials and rehearse.
- Identify next steps beyond the semester.

### Suggested sequence
- **Week 7 (30 Nov–6 Dec):** Pitch deck and storytelling; value proposition; demo flow; first practice presentations.
- **Week 8 (7–13 Dec):** Demo rehearsal; peer feedback; final testing; submission-readiness review.
- **14 December:** Suggested programme closeout, showcase or retrospective. Confirm the format and time in the official groups.

### Milestone
Each team has a concise pitch, a tested demo or backup recording, a project repository with setup instructions where applicable, and a next-steps plan.

## Buildathon challenge cadence

- At least **two buildathon challenges per week** will be announced in the official groups.
- Challenges will rotate across Web Development, Cloud Computing, Mobile Development, AI & Machine Learning, and Cybersecurity.
- Each announcement should include: challenge title, track, objective, eligibility/team format, deadline, deliverables, submission method and evaluation criteria.
- Use the track files in [`../challenges/`](../challenges/) as templates. The organisers should clearly label each prompt as **Open**, **Closed**, or **Coming soon**.
- The weekly challenge cadence runs alongside the two-week learning blocks; members should check the groups for the active prompts.
